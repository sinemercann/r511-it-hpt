How Did IT and HPT Develop?

Instructional Technology

Teaching tools changed over time. So did the way people planned teaching. Reiser (2001a, 2001b) describes these changes using examples from the United States.

  1. Early 1900s · Pictures and films

    Teachers used photos, slides, and films to show places and events that students could not see in class. Later, radio and television helped lessons reach people outside the classroom (Reiser, 2001a).

  2. 1940s–1950s · Training and learning goals

    During World War II, many people needed training quickly. This encouraged people to plan training step by step. In the 1950s, B. F. Skinner used small steps and quick feedback. Benjamin Bloom and his colleagues grouped learning goals by the kinds of thinking they require, such as remembering or using an idea (Reiser, 2001b).

  3. 1960s–1970s · Instructional design models

    Design models brought learning goals, methods, materials, and assessment together. Robert Mager stressed that a goal should say what a learner can do after a lesson. Designers also tested materials with learners and used the results to improve them (Reiser, 2001b).

  4. 1980s–1990s · Computers and the internet

    Computers gave students more ways to practice at their own speed and get feedback. The internet made it easier to share course materials and communicate from a distance. Designers also paid more attention to what learners already knew and how they understood new ideas (Reiser, 2001a, 2001b).

Human Performance Technology

HPT grew from work on how to help people do their jobs better. In 1978, Thomas F. Gilbert presented the Behavior Engineering Model in Human Competence. The model looks at what people know and can do and at the support they get at work, such as clear instructions, useful feedback, and the right tools (Gilbert, 1978).

If teachers have trouble using a school platform, the team can ask if they need more practice, clearer instructions, or better access. The answer helps the team choose support.
