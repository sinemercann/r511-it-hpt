IT and HPT in Schools

Four examples show how schools can use IT and HPT in everyday situations.

Math hints

IT

Situation. Fifth-grade students click to see the answer before they try a hard problem.

What can we do? The teacher adds a hint such as “What information does the problem give you?” Students read it and try to solve the problem.

How can we check? The teacher checks if students make a new attempt and if they can solve and explain a similar problem without help.

Shared tablets

HPT

Situation. Shared tablets are sometimes out of charge or still in another classroom.

What can we do? The school team asks how teachers book, return, and charge the tablets. If the rules are unclear, the team adds a shared calendar and a sign to remind teachers to plug in the tablets.

How can we check? The team compares how many class activities were delayed before and after the change.

Lunch waste

IT and HPT

Situation. Students put food scraps, drinks, and packaging in the same bin.

What can we do? With IT, the teacher uses photos to help students practice sorting. With HPT, the team watches what happens when students return trays. If the layout causes confusion, the team moves the bins and adds picture labels.

How can we check? The teacher checks sorting in class. The team tracks how often items go into the wrong bin at lunch.

Digital portfolios

IT and HPT

Situation. Teachers upload student work, but it is hard to see how the work improves over time.

What can we do? With HPT, the school team sets clear expectations and makes time for portfolio work. With IT, teachers show students how to compare two versions of their work. Students explain what they changed and what they want to improve.

How can we check? The team checks if portfolios include both versions. Teachers check if students can explain their changes.
